A0. THREE FAILURE MODES — align on these before anything else  [O]
    We must be on the same page about what problems we're solving:

    1. STRICT CONTAMINATION — same example literally in both sets.
       Fix: n-gram overlap check (script). Trivial.
    2. IN-DISTRIBUTION OVERFITTING — same authors, same instincts;
       model pattern-matches to distribution shape without understanding.
       Fix: different authors for train vs eval; diverse eval; embedding
       similarity checks.
    3. POOR GENERALIZATION — model can't reason about tool selection on
       genuinely novel query shapes it wasn't trained on.
       Fix: compositional held-out splits; explicit OOD eval tier;
       adversarial subsets.

    and #3 NOT AT ALL. That is the load-bearing gap in our methodology.

A1. TRAINING DATA SOURCE  [L → discuss impl]
    Decision (Marv): "im going with the independent external dataset
    with review and scripting output adaptation for eval process."

    What this means:
    - Training data = a pre-existing external dataset (ToolBench,
      API-Bank, or similar), NOT written by us
    - Manual review = sample-check for quality, not full curation
    - Output adaptation script = translation layer that converts what
      the fine-tuned model emits (in the external dataset's format)
      into BAYMAX's AgentResponse shape before your scorer sees it

    Why this path (me): eliminates author bias (data written by people
    who have never seen your eval); no LLM-mode-collapse concern (data
    is human-authored/labeled at scale); output adapter reframes the
    "wrong tool namespace" problem from a data problem to an engineering
    problem.

    Rejected path (LLM-generation): after review realised that "it is
    very likely that an llm making 2000 scenarios will build very simple
    similar scenarios" — LLMs mode-collapse under bulk generation.
    Documented in Wang 2022 (Self-Instruct), Xu 2023 (Evol-Instruct),
    GPT-4 tech report contamination section.

    Discussion needed:
    - Which external dataset? ToolBench (largest, general) vs API-Bank
      (curated, narrower) vs multi-source. Your SFT experience decides.
    - Adapter script ownership — my side (runtime) or yours (post-
      processing before scorer)?
    - Coverage gap enumeration — for BAYMAX-unique behaviors (refusal,
      clarification, _contains, verification) that don't exist in
      external data, we need a supplementary training set. Small
      (~100-200 template-generated), narrowly scoped.

    IMPORTANT ACKNOWLEDGMENT: expected outcome with this approach is
    that the fine-tune UNDERPERFORMS gpt-4o-mini baseline by 5-15
    points. Adapter is brittle at boundaries. This is a known cost of
    the approach. Our research narrative must be prepared to own it —
    see A6 below.

A3. EVAL DESIGN GAPS — five things to fix  [O] youve read docs u understand this
    Outside-critic pass on current 50-scenario eval flagged:

    (i) VOLUME IS THIN
        50 scenarios → ~10-15% noise on proportion metrics. A 5-point
        score difference between backends could be pure noise. Need 200+
        for defensible relative-quality claims; 500+ for small-difference
        claims. Your roadmap targets 200 by Phase 3 — is that pullable
        forward given extended timeline?

    (ii) NO STATED DIVERSITY STRUCTURE
         Are the 50 structurally different or 50 variants of ~5 patterns?
         Ask: 20-min diversity audit. Classify each scenario by
         (tool combo, intent type, phrasing style, error mode). If they
         fall into <5 buckets, effective diversity is much lower than
         50 suggests.

    (iii) NO COMPOSITIONAL HELD-OUT SPLIT
          Standard practice: train on tools A+B and A+C individually;
          eval includes B+C combos never seen in training. Tests whether
          model learned tool semantics vs memorized combinations.
          Ask: can we structure this in v1 or is it a v2 concern?

    (iv) NO OOD TIER
         Rigorous eval design has an explicit out-of-distribution subset
         (deliberately novel phrasings, adversarial edge cases,
         unfamiliar tool combinations). Reported SEPARATELY from
         in-distribution score.
         Ask: reserve 10-20 of the target-200 scenarios as OOD; author
         them separately from any training-data-adjacent workflow.

    (v) NO PRE-REGISTERED FAILURE CRITERION
        What result counts as "the fine-tune didn't work"? Agree BEFORE
        running the experiment. Without this, any outcome gets
        rationalized as success and reviewers see through it.

    >>> PRE-MEETING NOTE (from advance chat with Ronin):
    Ronin asked: "is a template with strong cases and categorisation
    enough to generate the 150 new scenarios, or do I need to make
    them myself?"

    Answer: HYBRID. Template + LLM handles the bulk cheaply; you
    hand-author the sharp cases where LLM is known to fail.

    Recommended split for 150 new scenarios:
    - ~100-120 IN-DISTRIBUTION + COMPOSITIONAL (Template + LLM + you
      spot-review):
      Template enumerates buckets explicitly (tool combos, intent
      types, difficulty tiers) → LLM generates variations → guarantees
      categorical coverage. Cheap.
    - ~15-20 OOD tier (you hand-author):
      Novel phrasings, unusual tool combos. LLMs generate "typical,"
      not novel — this is where template+LLM systematically fails.
    - ~10-15 ADVERSARIAL subset (you hand-author):
      Genuinely tricky, ambiguous, contradictory inputs. LLMs cannot
      generate real adversarial cases — they over-explain and hedge.
    - ~5-10 REFUSAL / CLARIFICATION / VERIFICATION (you hand-author):
      LLMs bias toward "helpful/succeed" and miss these categories
      entirely. Also happens to be where the baseline is weakest
      (refusal 21%, clarification 55%) → most valuable to test.

    Total hand-author burden: ~30-45 scenarios = ~8-12 hours of your
    focused work. Vs 30-50 hours doing all 150 by hand. Big saving
    without sacrificing quality where it matters.

    Post-generation, YOU also need to:
    - Spot-review 10-20 random LLM-generated ones for
      expected_behavior correctness (~1-2 hrs). LLM may set wrong
      "expected" and train the eval to reward wrong things.
    - Run diversity metrics to confirm bucket distribution landed
      (~30 min script).
    - Embedding-similarity check between generated and hand-authored
      batches to catch accidental overlap.

    Note on template design: I (Marv) should NOT write the template —
    my knowledge of what the fine-tune will be trained on would leak
    into the template's bucket definitions. You write the template,
    generation prompt, and hand-author the sharp cases. I stay out of
    eval authorship entirely. Symmetric protection matching A2.

A5. VERIFICATION COST ACKNOWLEDGMENT  [O]
    From the convo: "however to assure that the training data and eval
    data isnt too similar would require human verification."

    Reality check on options:
    - n-gram overlap = free but only catches verbatim reuse
    - Embedding cosine similarity = cheap, catches ~60-70% of paraphrase
    - Full human pairwise review = 100K pairs for 50 eval × 2000 train =
      140 hours; infeasible
    - Sample-based statistical review = 1-2 hours; produces "estimated
      overlap rate = X%" but not per-scenario guarantee

    Chosen approach mitigates this: since training data is external and
    we haven't seen it during eval authoring, semantic overlap risk is
    much lower than same-author scenarios would be. Still needs a
    sample-based check on the OUTPUT ADAPTER — did we accidentally
    encode eval-specific patterns in the translation table?


A6. COMPARISON FRAMING — pick the headline claim  [O]
    Head-to-head "small fine-tune beats gpt-4o-mini" is nearly
    impossible to defend cleanly. Small model = specialist trained on
    narrow data; frontier = generalist trained on internet. Unfair
    comparison.

    Defensible alternatives (pick 1-2 for headline):
    - A. Fine-tune vs its own base (Qwen 1.5B untuned vs Qwen 1.5B +
         our fine-tune) — measures fine-tuning delta cleanly
    - B. Fine-tune vs same-tier baseline (a published 1-2B tool-use
         fine-tune, e.g., Phi-2 or TinyLlama on ToolBench) — like-for-
         like at parameter tier
    - C. Cost-normalized (gpt-4o-mini cost per scenario vs local Qwen
         ≈ $0) — "achieves N% quality at 0% cost" story
    - D. Transfer-learning ceiling (our chosen approach!) — characterize
         how far external-data training gets on a domain-specific
         contract. Publishable as ablation/negative-space study.

    Recommendation from me: run A + D as parallel headline claims.
    Both defensible. Neither requires the fine-tune to win.
